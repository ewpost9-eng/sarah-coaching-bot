# Sarah's method and rubric

Use this reference for every drill or critique. It captures the behavior of the original Sarah Coaching Lab without reproducing its application code, user history, or private data.

## The organizing lens

The Foursquare framework is a live conversational map, not a four-step checklist. Choose the next move from the client's present need, and move backward when resistance, emotion, or new meaning appears.

| | Thought | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Support** | Develop understanding through connection, reflective observation, narrative clarification, meaning, and agenda. | Strengthen autonomy, values, readiness, confidence, and the client's own reasons for change. |
| **Challenge** | Clarify a well-formed outcome; examine assumptions, evidence, perspectives, beliefs, and self-talk. | Generate client-owned options, experiments, commitments, measures, obstacle planning, support, and accountability. |

### Support for Thought

Stay with sense-making before testing beliefs or designing action. Listen for one or more forms of understanding:

- **Contributing Conditions:** context, influences, sequence, or triggers shaping the experience.
- **Self-Understanding:** personal reactions, needs, motives, and characteristic responses.
- **Recurring Pattern:** thoughts, feelings, behaviors, or dynamics repeated over time.
- **Cross-Situational Meaning:** how a pattern operates across roles, relationships, or settings.
- **Personal Contribution:** how choices, habits, or responses help maintain the situation, without blame.
- **Consequences and Significance:** what the experience produces and why it matters.
- **Emerging Direction:** an early wish or forward pull without turning it into a plan too soon.

These are recognition categories, not stages. Several may appear together. Evaluating or replacing a belief moves toward Challenge for Thought; constructing a plan moves toward Action.

Useful invitations include:

- Contributing Conditions: “What surrounding conditions seem to be shaping this experience?”
- Self-Understanding: “What are you learning about how you tend to respond here?”
- Recurring Pattern: “Where have you noticed this pattern repeating?”
- Cross-Situational Meaning: “How does this show up in other parts of your leadership or life?”
- Personal Contribution: “How might your own response be shaping what continues?”
- Consequences and Significance: “What is this producing, and why does that matter to you?”
- Emerging Direction: “As you reflect on this, what direction is beginning to emerge?”

Common novice errors: rushing to reframe, goals, or advice; asking a question without first meeting the feeling or meaning; and assigning the coach's interpretation to the client.

### Challenge for Thought

Help the client make outcomes observable and personally meaningful, examine the story they are telling, test evidence, surface assumptions or self-talk, and consider a new perspective. Challenge should increase awareness, not prosecute the client or force the coach's hypothesis.

### Support for Action

Explore importance, values, ambivalence, readiness, and confidence. Evoke the client's own reasons for change and respect autonomy. Avoid the righting reflex, cheerleading, or selling a plan; these can produce compliant language without ownership.

### Challenge for Action

Help the client generate options, choose a feasible experiment, define what and when, anticipate obstacles, identify support, and decide how learning will be reviewed. Accountability is client-designed, not surveillance. A useful experiment can create learning even when the result is imperfect.

## Five-part scoring heuristic

Score each dimension from 0 to 20. Use the whole range sparingly and anchor the explanation in the learner's exact response. The total is formative only.

- **Presence:** attunement, restraint, brevity, emotional contact, and room for the client.
- **Listening:** accurate use of the client's words, meaning, tension, and emotion rather than a generic response.
- **Inquiry:** one focused, open invitation that deepens discovery; closed, stacked, leading, or interrogative questions score lower.
- **Quadrant fit:** the move addresses the conversational need without moving to challenge or action too soon.
- **Client ownership:** autonomy, partnership, choice, and discovery remain with the client rather than the coach.

A concise reflection plus one purposeful open question is often strong, but never score the surface formula without testing whether it fits this client moment.

## Supporting approaches in the original project

These approaches complement the Foursquare map; they are not separate required sequences:

- **ICF-aligned behaviors and ethics:** presence, listening, partnership, awareness, growth, agreements, confidentiality, boundaries, competence, and client ownership shape the rubric and red-flag cases. The rubric is not an ICF assessment.
- **Motivational interviewing:** ambivalence, autonomy, change talk, importance, confidence, and avoidance of the righting reflex are especially useful in Support for Action.
- **GROW:** awareness, options, responsibility, and will offer a practical comparison for outcome and action work, without forcing every conversation through a fixed order.
- **Solution-focused coaching:** preferred futures, exceptions, scaling, and resource-oriented questions can sharpen well-formed outcomes and client-generated possibilities.
- **Cognitive and behavioral ideas:** self-talk, evidence, beliefs, alternative frames, and behavioral experiments support Challenge for Thought and Challenge for Action.
- **Competing commitments:** protective assumptions and feared consequences can explain why a stated goal and repeated behavior conflict.
- **Working alliance and coaching psychology:** relationship quality, psychological foundations, context, competence, and the boundary with therapy inform presence and ethics.
- **Coach self-management:** notice the urge to rescue, impress, diagnose, agree, or move quickly; those reactions often distort quadrant choice.

Treat all of these as evidence-informed resources rather than proof that one intervention is uniquely correct. Use neuroscience only as cautious explanatory context, never as intervention proof.

## Calibration signals

Treat these as evidence, not mechanical rules:

- Advice-like language includes “you should,” “you need to,” “why don't you,” “I would,” “have you tried,” or a disguised solution.
- More than two questions usually overloads the client; prefer one clean question.
- A closed opening narrows exploration unless a deliberate confirmation is needed.
- Support scenarios usually benefit from an accurate reflection or acknowledgment before inquiry.
- Very long responses can crowd the client; very short questions can feel disconnected from what was said.
- Ethical awareness overrides technique when confidentiality, sponsor agreements, conflicts of interest, competence, referral, harm, abuse, or legal/HR process is material.

## Client-reaction calibration

- **Receptive:** feels heard and retains ownership; opens up or chooses a direction.
- **Considering:** engages but may need sharper reflection or focus.
- **Guarded:** manages the coach's agenda, complies politely, or redirects to what was missed.
- **Withdrawn:** safety, scope, or partnership has broken down.

Never caricature the fictional client. Phrase the interpretation as “may,” “might,” or “one plausible reaction.”

## Scenario design

Use fictional senior-leadership contexts such as healthcare operations, product leadership, people leadership, finance, nonprofit leadership, engineering, revenue leadership, or higher education. Vary identity, communication style, organizational setting, and the tension between inner experience and external demands.

Difficulty can rise through ambiguity, emotionally charged meaning, competing stakeholder demands, client resistance, or an ethical layer. Ethics cases may include:

- a sponsor requesting a private summary outside the coaching agreement;
- a request to reveal another person's confidential conversation;
- panic attacks or serious mental-health concerns that may require referral;
- a client asking the coach to decide whom to terminate; or
- imminent harm, abuse, or a legal/HR investigation requiring qualified human support.

Do not use a real person's details. Do not make cultural difference itself the defect being scored.
